Overview

Over the course of my project I made 345 observations, covering 198 unique plant species. These species span across 53 families and were recorded at 4 different sites: Beechwood, Coton, Mill Road Cemetery and Wandlebury.

Wandlebury has the highest species richness, suggesting it is the most biodiverse site sampled. Beechwood has the fewest unique species, suggesting it is the least biodiverse. After having done some research, I discovered that Wandlebury is by far the oldest site, with an approximate age of 2,300 years, 2,100 years older than the next oldest site. This could suggest why it has the most biodiverse flora. Coton stands out for the highest concentration of a single family. Asteraceae makes up nearly 20% of its observations, well above the other sites.

Plants shared across sites

Of the 198 species observed, 148 (74%) were found at only one site. Most plants are fairly localised to a single location. Only 5 species were observed at all 4 sites:

  • Bindweed (Convolvulus arvensis L.)
  • Lady's bedstraw (Galium verum L.)
  • Pruinose Bramble (Rubus pruinosus)
  • Stinging nettle (Urtica dioica L.)
  • Dwarf nettle (Urtica urens L.)

All five of these species are common weeds, which makes ecological sense as they are ubiquitous species.

Takeaway

The four sites share a common background of common species such as nettles, bindweed, bedstraw. However, each site has a distinct set of locally dominant, unique, largely non-overlapping species. My research has shown the variety of environments and plant ecosystems that exist within the Cambridge area.
